# EnteleBANK — Financial Crime Risk Management Framework v0.1

Status: Working internal regulatory document. Not evidence of authorisation or operational readiness.

## Objective
Design a proportionate, risk-based financial-crime control environment before regulated services are launched.

## Risk universe
- money laundering and terrorist financing;
- sanctions and proliferation-financing exposure;
- fraud, scams and account misuse;
- bribery and corruption;
- tax-evasion facilitation;
- mule activity and organised financial crime;
- identity/document fraud and synthetic identities;
- cyber-enabled financial crime;
- insider threat and collusion;
- correspondent/cross-border risk where applicable.

## Control lifecycle
1. Enterprise-wide financial crime risk assessment.
2. Customer/product/channel/geography risk taxonomy.
3. Customer due diligence and identity verification design.
4. Beneficial-owner/control verification for legal persons.
5. Enhanced due diligence triggers and approval authorities.
6. PEP, sanctions and adverse-information screening design.
7. Transaction-monitoring/scenario governance.
8. Fraud/scam prevention and intervention controls.
9. Alert investigation, case management and escalation.
10. Suspicious-activity reporting governance.
11. Record retention and evidence integrity.
12. Management information, QA, testing and independent assurance.

## Customer onboarding principles
No production onboarding flow will be enabled until the legal basis, permissions, policies, risk appetite, vendor due diligence, data protection, control testing and accountable owners are approved. The target journey must support appropriate identity verification, source-of-funds/source-of-wealth measures where risk requires, customer risk rating, screening and ongoing monitoring.

## Sanctions
The framework must support applicable UK sanctions obligations and any other legally applicable regimes based on customer/product/geographic perimeter. Screening configuration, list sources, matching thresholds, escalation, licensing/exemption handling and change management require documented governance.

## Transaction monitoring
Scenarios must map to documented risks and products. Thresholds cannot be treated as static defaults. Calibration, effectiveness testing, false-positive analysis, typology updates and model/rule change controls must be evidenced.

## Fraud and scams
Fraud controls should be integrated with onboarding, authentication, device/session intelligence, payment risk, behavioural monitoring, beneficiary risk, customer warnings/interventions and post-event response. Customer outcome considerations must be included alongside loss prevention.

## Governance
Target accountable functions: Board oversight; executive risk ownership; MLRO/financial-crime leadership subject to final SMCR design; first-line control owners; independent second-line challenge; independent assurance/audit. Names and appointments remain evidence-gated.

## Outsourcing and technology
Third-party identity, screening, monitoring or case-management services do not transfer regulatory accountability. Selection requires security, resilience, data, concentration, subcontracting, exit and audit-right analysis.

## Evidence required before readiness claims
- approved financial-crime risk assessment;
- approved policies/standards/procedures;
- accountable role appointments and competence evidence;
- documented vendor selection/due diligence;
- scenario/rule inventory and rationale;
- test evidence and defect closure;
- training and competence records;
- MI/KRI definitions and escalation thresholds;
- independent review/challenge;
- incident and regulatory-reporting procedures.

## Sensitive-name guardrail
Nothing in this document permits public claims that EnteleBANK currently performs KYC, monitoring, sanctions screening or regulated banking. Public wording remains governed by Issue #9.
