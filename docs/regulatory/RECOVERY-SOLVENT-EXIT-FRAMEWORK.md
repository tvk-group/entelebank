# EnteleBANK Recovery & Solvent Exit Framework v0.1

**Status:** Internal working framework — pre-authorisation  
**Public claim:** None. This document does not imply authorisation or operational readiness.

## 1. Objective
Establish an evidence-led framework for restoring viability under severe stress and, if viability cannot credibly be restored, executing an orderly solvent exit while protecting customers and limiting disruption.

## 2. Governance
Before application readiness the framework must identify accountable Board/executive ownership, escalation thresholds, decision rights, independent challenge, management-information requirements and communication responsibilities. Names and SMF allocations remain pending until appointments are evidenced.

## 3. Recovery indicators
Candidate indicator families include capital, liquidity, funding concentration, deposit/customer behaviour, asset quality, profitability/runway, operational availability, cyber/security, financial crime, third-party dependency and reputational/conduct indicators. Thresholds must be calibrated from the approved business and prudential models; no thresholds are invented here.

## 4. Recovery options
Candidate options to assess for feasibility, speed, dependencies and side effects include cost reduction, capital raising, liquidity actions, balance-sheet reduction, funding diversification, restriction of growth/new business, asset disposal, service simplification and group support where legally documented and credible.

Each option requires evidence of execution time, legal constraints, operational capacity, customer/conduct impact, financial effect and interaction with other options.

## 5. Severe scenarios
Scenario design must align with the five-year model and operational-resilience framework. It should include idiosyncratic, market-wide and combined stress, cyber/technology disruption, third-party failure, financial-crime/fraud event and funding/liquidity stress.

## 6. Solvent exit
The solvent-exit plan must define triggers for considering exit, governance and decision-making, funding/runway through exit, customer communications, deposit/account migration or closure mechanics where applicable, treatment of complaints and vulnerable customers, contracts/outsourcing termination, data retention/deletion, staff dependencies, regulatory engagement and legal-entity wind-down.

No assumption may be made that another institution will acquire customers, assets or liabilities unless supported by a credible arrangement or tested market evidence.

## 7. Operational continuity during exit
Important activities needed to execute an orderly exit must remain supportable for the required period. Dependency maps must identify technology, cloud, payments, identity/KYC, fraud/financial-crime, customer support, finance/treasury, records and key people required through wind-down.

## 8. Financial resources
The financial model must eventually quantify recovery-option impacts and solvent-exit costs/runway under evidence-backed assumptions. The framework does not state a required amount today.

## 9. Testing
Recovery and exit playbooks require tabletop exercises, scenario testing, decision simulations and remediation tracking before they can be described as operationally credible.

## 10. Sensitive-name control
Nothing in this document changes EnteleBANK's public status: it remains a proposed institution under development and is not currently authorised by the PRA/FCA to accept deposits or provide regulated banking services. Issue #9 remains the public-release guardrail.
