# EnteleBANK Permissions & Conduct Readiness Framework v0.1

**Status:** Internal working framework — pre-authorisation

## Objective
Create a controlled mapping from the proposed business model and products to the permissions, customer protections, conduct obligations and evidence that would be required before any regulated service is offered.

## Permission-by-activity mapping
Every proposed product/capability must be decomposed into actual activities rather than inferred from marketing labels. The future permissions matrix should identify activity, customer type, channel, legal entity, applicable regulatory perimeter, dependencies, required controls, launch prerequisites and evidence owner.

No permission is treated as granted until evidenced by the relevant regulator/register.

## Product approval
Before any future launch, product governance should evidence target market, customer need, foreseeable harm, distribution, eligibility, pricing/value, disclosures, support, vulnerability considerations, fraud/scam risk, complaints, operational readiness, financial-crime controls and post-launch monitoring.

## Consumer Duty
For retail products, the target framework must demonstrate outcomes across products/services, price/value, consumer understanding and consumer support. Evidence should include testing of communications and journeys, fair-value assessment, support accessibility, vulnerability treatment, outcome metrics and remediation/escalation.

## Customer communications
Public and customer-facing language must be fair, clear and not misleading. Pre-authorisation communications must distinguish proposed services from available services and must not imply authorisation, FSCS/FOS applicability or regulatory endorsement where those do not apply.

## Vulnerable customers
Design should identify potential vulnerability, provide accessible channels and reasonable support, avoid harmful friction, train relevant staff and monitor whether outcomes differ materially for vulnerable cohorts.

## Complaints and remediation
Before customer launch, establish complaint intake, ownership, investigation, response, root-cause analysis, redress/remediation, management information, escalation and recordkeeping aligned to the final permissions and applicable rules.

## Financial promotions
Any future promotion must be classified and approved under the applicable regime before publication. Marketing approval cannot be inferred from brand/name approval or from a website disclaimer.

## Cross-border controls
Availability by geography requires legal/regulatory assessment. A translated website does not mean services are offered or authorised in that language's country. Locale availability and product availability must remain separate controls.

## Launch gate
A regulated product cannot move from proposed to available until its permission, governance, capital/liquidity implications, financial-crime controls, technology/resilience, customer support, complaints, legal terms, data/privacy, third parties, testing and Board-approved launch evidence are complete.

## Sensitive-name boundary
FCA non-objection to a sensitive business name, if obtained, is not banking authorisation and must never be represented as such. Issue #9 remains release-blocking.
